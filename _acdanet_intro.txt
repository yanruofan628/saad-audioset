From Neural Representations to Brain-Inspired 
Architecture: Decoding Auditory Target Perception in 
Complex Scenes 
Jianting Shi, Luzheng Bi*, Jiaqi Wang, Jiarong Wang, Aberham Genetu Feleke and Weijie Fei 
 
Abstract— In complex acoustic scenarios, the human 
auditory system excels at rapidly and accurately identifying 
target sounds. A deeper understanding of its mechanisms in 
such environments could significantly enhance the 
robustness and generalization capabilities of  sound event 
detection systems. This paper investigates the neural 
representations and decoding of target sound perception in 
complex auditory scenes using non-invasive neural signals. 
We propose a novel experimental paradigm that simulates 
complex acoustic conditions by varying parameters such as 
target sound characteristics, background noise levels, and 
interfering events. Multi- view neural representations —
including time, frequency, and source domains— are 
extracted and analyzed using statistical methods to examine 
their relationships with these variables. To decode these 
neural activities, we propose the Auditory Cortex- inspired 
Dual Attention Network (AC -DANet), an architecture 
functionally inspired by known attentional pathways in the 
auditory cortex. The model achieves robust three -class 
Electroencephalogram (EEG) decoding for target sound 
perception in challenging auditory scenes, with experimental 
results demonstrating strong performance and cross -
subject generalization. This study advances our 
understanding of the neural information transmission 
process underlying sound target perception in complex 
acoustic environments. It offers novel insights into the 
cognitive functions of the human auditory system, while 
providing a theoretical foundation and technical framework 
for the development of advanced sound event detection 
systems in challenging acoustic settings. 
Index Terms—Sound Target Perception, Complex 
Acoustic Scenario , Low SNR , EEG, Noninvasive Neural 
Signals, Neural signature.

To date, the fundamental composition of the auditory 
cognitive pathway has been preliminarily established [9]. The 
mainstream view is that the human auditory pathway is 
composed of the cochlea, thalamus and brain stem, auditory 
cortex and higher-order cognitive areas of the brain. Currently, 
several studies have explored the specific transmission process of 
auditory information in auditory pathways during the perception 
of auditory targets. 
Tsunada et al. [10]  summarized part of the research on the 
neural mechanism of human auditory content classification and 
pointed out that the human a uditory cortex is divided into core 
auditory cortex and belt region. When classifying the heard sound, 
neurons in the core auditory cortex encode simple sounds, while 
when sounds become more complex, the core auditory cortex 
encodes their acoustic features and complex sounds are classified 
by the belt area and the ventral prefrontal cortex. Helene et al. [11] 
pointed out in their article that auditory target perception includes 
two processes, namely, lowering the detection threshold of target 
sound and actively filtering irrelevant sound. These two 
processes not only require the participation of auditory cortex, 
but also affect the frontal cortex, bilateral precentral area, left 
postcentral area and supplementary motor area of the brain. 
Through the study of Magnetoencephalography (MEG) 
information in the area near the auditory cortex, Ding et al. [12] 
pointed out that the neural representation of the auditory cortex 
would remain phase-locked with the envelope of the target sound 
and have a similar waveform to the envelope of the target sound 
in complex sound scenes. Elena et al. [13]  used invasive 
electroencephalogram (ECoG) to further investigate the selective 
attention of human hearing, which involved the neural signal 
components of two frequency bands. Among them, the neurons 
with selectivity to the target sound were mainly distributed in the 
prefrontal lobe and the precentral area. It also confirms the role 
of the hierarchical structure of the auditory pathway and the top-
down neuromodulation mechanism in auditory selective 
attention. Wang et al. [14]  used Electroencephalogram ( EEG)

grounded in physiological mechanisms can achieve robust 
generalization. This work provides not only theoretical support

more interpretable and effective brain -computer interfaces 
designed for real-world environments. 
II.
 PARADIGM AND DATA ACQUISITION 
A. Experimental Paradigm and Procedure 
Twelve healthy, gender -balanced subjects (aged 22- 27) 
participated in a target perception experiment designed to 
simulate complex acoustic scenes. The study was conducted in 
accordance with the 2013 Declaration of Helsinki and received 
ethical approval from the Research Ethics Committee of 
Beijing Institute of Technology (No. BIT -EC-H-2024150). All 
participants provided written informed consent and confirmed 
they had no history of neurological diseases nor had recently 
consumed substances that could affect the results, such as 
alcohol, tobacco, or psychoactive drugs.  Fig. 1. is a schematic 
diagram of the paradigm for the sound target perception 
experiment. Fig. 2. shows the experimental scenario. 
Each experimental trial consisted of 50 sound clips (1000ms 
duration, 200ms inter -stimulus interval), presented in a 
randomized order. Within each trial, 30 clips contained only 
background wind noise, while the remaining 20 contained 
embedded sound events. Of these events, two were designated 
as targets and 18 as interferers. The acoustic events were 
sourced from the UrbanSound8K dataset [17 ] and included ten 
categories of everyday sounds (e.g., car horn, dog bark, siren). 
"Gunshot" served as the target event, with the other nine 
categories acting as interferers. To simulate complex acoustic 
environments, all sound events were embedded in bac kground 
wind noise at two signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs): high (0dB) and 
low (-10dB), with an equal number of events presented in each 
condition. Stimuli were delivered binaurally through 
headphones (Sony WH-1000XM5, Japan). 
Participants were fitted with an EEG cap and seated in front 
of a display in a quiet room. Each trial began with a 5- second 
preparation period. During the stimulus presentation, 
participants were instructed to maintain fixation on the screen's 
center and p ress the 'space' key as quickly as possible upon 
hearing the target sound. The system recorded stimulus onsets, 
key presses, and reaction times (RT s), which were 
synchronized with the EEG data via serial communication. 
Each subject completed 75 trials, with a 1 -minute rest period 
between each trial. The entire experimental paradigm was 
designed and implemented using PsychoPy [18]. 
B. Data Acquisition 
We utilized the 64-channel EEG amplifier system (NeuroScan 
SynAmps2, NeuroScan, America) to acquire EEG signals from 
participants. The electrode positions were determined according 
to the international 10 -20 system standard, with the forehead 
ground electrode placed at AFz and the reference electrode at 
CPz. The sampling rate was set at 1000 Hz to ensure hig h 
temporal resolution of neural activities. Additionally, electrode 
impedance was calibrated to less than 10 kΩ to minimize noise 
and enhance signal quality. 
To synchronize EEG recordings with the presentation of target 
sounds, we controlled the timing of sound presentation using 
PsychoPy and sent corresponding trigger signals to the EEG  
acquisition device via serial communication. This ensured 
precise alignment between neural activities and auditory stimuli.

III. DATA ANALYSIS 
A. EEG Preprocessing 
Baseline correction was applied to raw EEG signals (using the 
mean of the first 10% of each trial  to eliminate zero drift), and 
trials with maximum absolute amplitudes exceeding 200 μV 
were excluded. The signals were then down-sampled to 200 Hz 
and subjected to a FIR band-pass filter within the range of 0.5-49 
Hz. Subsequently, the ICA algorithm was applied to the data 
filtered between 2-49 Hz to remove ocular and muscular artifacts, 
with optimization based on [1 9]. The ICLabel algorithm was 
utilized to label and remove ocular and muscular components 
with a confidence level greater than 90% [20]. Finally, the EEG 
data were re-referenced to a common average reference (CAR).  
All of the above pre -processing processes were completed 
through EEGLAB [21]. 
B. Time-Domain Analysis 
We adopted a time-domain analysis method to deeply explore 
the EEG data in the target perception experiment in complex 
acoustic scenes. We averaged the extracted epochs to separate the 
stimulation-related event-related potential ( ERP) components 
from the background noise, thus more clearly revealing the 
differences in brain responses to target stimuli under different 
conditions. In the analysis of ERP components, we focused on  
the central region and the parietal lobe. As an important part of 
the cerebral cortex, the central region is closely related to a 
variety of sensory functions. The parietal lobe is more related to 
cognitive process. We quantitatively compared the ERP 
components in different conditions by analyzing the waveform 
characteristics, latency and amplitude of ERP. The differences in 
these parameters not only reflect the speed and intensity of brain 
response to target stimuli, but also reveal the synergistic 
mechanism of different regions for target perception in complex 
acoustic scenes.

E. AC-DANet 
To decode the perception of auditory targets in complex 
acoustic scenes from single-trial EEG data, we propose the AC-
DANet. The architectural design of AC-DANet is not based on a 
generic deep learning template; rather, it is fundamentally 
motivated by a detailed analysis of the underlying neural 
representations of auditory perception, which we will elaborate 
upon in the Results section (Sec. IV .B). The network's core 
structure is designed to mimic the functional specialization and 
synergistic interaction of the brain's ventral and dorsal attention 
pathways. The architecture, depicted in Fig. 3, comprises three 
principal stages: (1) Multi -modal Neural Representation 
Embedding, (2) a Dual -Pathway Attention Core, and (3) an 
Executive Decision Unit trained with a joint loss function.
